## §1 Headline — the five findings that most change what the wing looks like

**1. The wing is a HAND that also flies, and the grasping fingers must stay visible in the
silhouette.** This is the single most consistent decision across the two highest-budget dragon
wings ever built, and both studios arrived at it independently.

- **Smaug (Weta Digital).** In *An Unexpected Journey* Weta built a four-legged Smaug with wings
  on his back; for *The Desolation of Smaug* "the character had to be able to emote through his
  hands – which necessitated a return to a two-legged dragon with wings on his arms"
  `[S]` https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/meet-smaug/. Peter Jackson pushed for **extra fingers
  and a thumb onto the wing membrane** so Smaug "could effectively have hands," which "allowed him
  to negotiate his environment and get into threatening poses" `[S]` (same source). The final
  build: **three short fingers (thumb, index, middle) serve as hand and claws — these are the short
  ones visible when he grabs and climbs — while three further fingers are elongated to spread the
  wing** `[S]` (same source). Weta's own note on the cost: "He uses his shoulders like arms, but
  they are also wings, which complicated the rigging" `[S]`
  https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/meet-smaug/.
- **Drogon (Pixomondo).** The team "created a new approach on the wing hands," working from
  **fruit-bat reference**, defining a more complex bone system that let animation **bend and squash
  the palm**, with **the thumb bending forward while the remaining fingers aim backwards**. Against
  the bat they **shortened the thumb and shifted it up the wrist, away from the fingers and palm,
  specifically to avoid reading as a human hand** `[S]`
  https://www.artofvfx.com/game-of-thrones-season-6-sven-martin-vfx-supervisor-pixomondo/.

⇒ **The wrist is a punctuation mark, not a bend.** A short, forward-pointing, clawed digit cluster
at the carpal apex — opposed in direction to the swept-back spars — is what separates a designed
wing from a stretched membrane.

**2. The membrane is not leather, and treating it as leather is the most expensive-looking cheap
tell on the surface.** From the best artist-facing bat guide retrieved: the patagium "is filled
with blood vessels and lines of muscle tissue. It is very soft and stretchy, and **not leathery at
all**," it carries "lots of tiny hairs on it, and some bats have fur growing right on the wing"
`[S]` https://koryos.tumblr.com/post/143906829796/bats-an-anatomy-guide-for-artists-animators.
Pixomondo, designing the emaciated *Game of Thrones* dragons, "explored various ideas from dry and
nearly transparent versions showing **fine veins and arteries**" `[S]`
https://www.artofvfx.com/game-of-thrones-season-6-sven-martin-vfx-supervisor-pixomondo/. Backlit
bat membrane photographs as translucent skin over "a complex network of visible blood vessels"
`[S]` https://stockcake.com/s/wing-veins.

⇒ **The membrane's hero read is transmission, not reflection.** A wing lit only from the front is
a tarp; the wing that sells is the one the sun is behind.

**3. The forward sheet (propatagium) is the part artists leave out, and its absence is the
recognised amateur signature.** The dedicated critique blog states it flatly: the membrane between
shoulder and wrist is "**the most commonly left out part of membranous wing anatomy**" `[S]`
https://fantasy-anatomy-analyst.tumblr.com/post/636512556370083840/membranous-wing-editon-description-a-digital.
A general drawing guide independently names the same two failures together: "the most common mistake
is drawing the wings completely flat (no joints in the fingers) **and forgetting about the membrane
between the shoulder and the wrist**" `[S]`
https://jerwoodvisualarts.org/blog/how-to-draw-dragon-wings/. This converges exactly with
`DRAGON-ANATOMY-REFERENCE.md` §4.1/§4.6 (both real membrane lineages evolved a dedicated
leading-edge tensioner) — the anatomy and the art critique agree, which is rare.

**4. A folded membrane does not pleat. It shrinks.** The clearest artist statement retrieved:
"the whole membrane is like one giant stretchy piece of **spandex** — when all the digits are
extended the membrane gets stretched taut, but when the wing relaxes **the membrane retracts into
itself**. The edges **don't fold up like a piece of fabric**" `[S]`
https://www.tumblr.com/rah-bop/99106220976/do-you-have-any-tips-or-tricks-for-drawing-dragon.
Corroborated: "the wing membrane itself is flexible and will simply **shrink inwards, no pleated
folding**" `[S]`
https://www.tumblr.com/fantasy-anatomy-analyst/636784318876991488/my-previous-post-discussed-the-musculature-of.

⇒ The folded pose is a **closing fan of overlapping skin lobes gathered along the bones
(spanwise)**, never a curtain of vertical accordion pleats running across the chord.

**5. Ripple wavelength is the scale cue. It is a size dial, not a detail dial.** On the *Monster
Hunter* film Rathalos, MR. X's Creature FX dynamics work on the wings deliberately included
"**smaller scale ripples to indicate the size of Rathalos**" `[S]`
https://beforesandafters.com/2021/01/25/everything-you-need-to-know-about-making-the-monsters-of-monster-hunter/.
Image Engine, lead dragon animation facility on *Game of Thrones* S8: "wing membranes will **ripple
and fold** when the wings move or react to the wind," and the show's supervisors drew directly on
frames to "visualize those **wing membranes filled with air**" `[S]`
https://image-engine.com/case-studies/game-of-thrones-season-8/.

⇒ **Many short-wavelength, low-amplitude ripples = enormous. Few long slow waves = small.** A
wing whose membrane oscillates in 2–3 big waves has just told the audience the dragon is
bird-sized regardless of how many metres you claim for it.

### §2.2 Silhouette language — what reads POWERFUL vs FRAIL

| Dial | Powerful read | Frail read | Tag / source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Leading edge weight** | Emphasised, thickened, the **strongest line in the drawing** — "strong outer lines create a more forceful silhouette, and **emphasising leading edges gives wings power visually**" | LE drawn at the same weight as the membrane's interior lines | `[S]` https://jerwoodvisualarts.org/blog/how-to-draw-dragon-wings/ (search-index paraphrase) |
| **Joint landmarks** | Elbow, wrist and every MCP knuckle remain **visible bumps in the outline** — "when they remain visible, the wing feels functional and easy to understand" | Smooth arc from shoulder to tip; the arm is a hose | `[S]` https://jerwoodvisualarts.org/blog/how-to-draw-dragon-wings/ (surfaced in two searches; wording is the index's paraphrase) |
| **Widest chord location** | **Inboard**, at the arm-wing, tapering outboard | Widest at mid-span or uniform | prior art `DRAGON-ANATOMY-REFERENCE.md` §4.2 (armwing ≈52% of area) |
| **Membrane state** | **Filled with air** — visibly cupped, carrying load | Slack, or flat and tensionless | `[S]` https://image-engine.com/case-studies/game-of-thrones-season-8/ |
| **Foreshortening** | "A wing **angled toward the viewer appears more massive**"; "small torso details make the wing feel larger by contrast" | Wing presented flat-on and parallel to camera | `[S]` https://jerwoodvisualarts.org/blog/how-to-draw-dragon-wings/ (index paraphrase) |
| **Tip shape** | Pointed/swept reads **fast** (coursers, swifts); rounded reads **manoeuvrable/low-speed** (hawkers, dense-habitat fliers) — pick one deliberately, do not average them | An unresolved blunt stub, or a needle so thin it has no mass | `[S]` https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-13894-4 |
| **Tip claw** | Legitimate fantasy exaggeration with shipped precedent — Rathalos has "claws sculpted onto the **upper portion of the wing tips**" | — | `[S]` https://monsterhunter.fandom.com/wiki/Rathalos |
| **Spar count in outline** | Countable, repeated, decaying — knuckled LE, scalloped TE | 1–2 spars → two smooth curves at distance | prior art §4.1 |
| **Trailing edge** | Broken into **concave bays**, plus healed notches | A single taut straight hem | prior art §4.5 + §2.5 below |
| **Asymmetry** | "Slight asymmetry can make the dragon feel more dynamic"; **avoid identical sections** | Left/right and bay/bay mirror-perfect | `[S]` https://jerwoodvisualarts.org/blog/how-to-draw-dragon-wings/ |
| **Shoulder mass** | The muscle lives in the **torso** — "most of the muscle strength required for flying is actually in the torso: back muscles, shoulders, and pectoral muscles" | Muscle drawn onto the wing arm; a slim torso with a bulging bicep | `[S]` https://www.tumblr.com/fantasy-anatomy-analyst/760730061567590400/back-with-another-wyvern-that-ive-always-been |
| **Membrane hem thickness** | "**Lighter lines on the membrane help preserve a sense of thinness**" — the membrane must out-thin the bones | Membrane outlined at bone weight → reads as sheet metal | `[S]` https://jerwoodvisualarts.org/blog/how-to-draw-dragon-wings/ (index paraphrase) |
| **Ripple wavelength** | Short wavelength, many cycles → huge | 2–3 long lazy waves → small | `[S]` https://beforesandafters.com/2021/01/25/everything-you-need-to-know-about-making-the-monsters-of-monster-hunter/ |
| **Motion blur / edge softness** | A **blurred wing edge** implies just-landed or about-to-launch | Every edge crisp in every frame | `[S]` https://www.creativebloq.com/digital-art/8-pro-tips-drawing-dragons-71515935 |

### §2.3 Proportion conventions, and where artists deliberately break from bat anatomy

| Convention | Value / statement | Load-bearing? | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wingspan vs body | "Wingspan should equal **two to three times the body length** from neck base to tail tip" — an illustrator rule of thumb, **not** a published measurement | Yes as a floor: the fell-beast critique is that wings must "look genuinely functional in comparison to the body size" | `[S]` https://jerwoodvisualarts.org/blog/how-to-draw-dragon-wings/ ; treat the number as an art rule, `[no-assert]` as biology |
| Digit count | Bats: 5 digits, pointer shortened. **Smaug: 6 (3 short + 3 spar). Drogon: thumb + several rearward fingers.** Both hero wings **added digits over the bat** | Yes — the extra short digits are what make the wrist act | `[S]` fxguide + artofvfx (see §2.1) |
| Thumb position | Bat/real: thumb free and clawed at the wrist. **Pixomondo moved it *up* the wrist and shortened it** to kill the human-hand read | Yes — a long thumb at the palm makes the wing look like a hand in a glove | `[S]` https://www.artofvfx.com/game-of-thrones-season-6-sven-martin-vfx-supervisor-pixomondo/ |
| Claws on spar fingers | Real: "there are **no claws** on the ends of any of the fingers besides the thumb," except Pteropodids, which have one on the shortened pointer | **Break carefully.** Rathalos ships a tip claw; the critique blog calls loose "weird wing claws" a defect. | Hindlimb attachment | Real bats run the membrane to the ankle. ### §2.4 The wing's ACTING — a named pose vocabulary

A wing that only flaps is a dead wing. Each of these is a **distinct silhouette**, not a mood.

| Pose | What it looks like | Real-world / studio anchor | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mantle** | Wings half-spread and **hunched forward and DOWN over something on the ground**, shoulders rolled forward, head lowered inside the tent. The wings become a hood that hides what is under them | Falconry term. A raptor "spreads its wings and haunches **over its prey**… likely a way to prevent other raptors or animals from **seeing and stealing** their food." Instinctive, learned in the nest to hide food from siblings | `[S]` https://www.themodernapprentice.com/behavior.htm ; https://ccbbirds.org/2009/06/27/the-mantle-vs-to-mantle/ |
| **Horaltic pose / full display spread** | Wings **stretched to their full length**, held open and static, body squared to the viewer, maximum projected area | The vulture sunning posture; "the most recognisable is the extended posture in which the wings are stretched to their full length" | `[S]` https://www.newyorkalmanack.com/2022/05/sunbathing-birds/ ; https://enviroliteracy.org/why-do-vultures-stand-with-their-wings-open/ |
| **Deimatic flare** | A **sudden** transition from small to enormous — the whole value of the pose is in the *rate of change*, not the end pose | "Threat display or startle display: any pattern of bluffing behaviour… **suddenly** displaying conspicuous features" | `[S]` https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deimatic_behaviour |
| **The heraldic frame** (the Drogon shot) | Wings spread symmetrically **behind** a figure so the wing outline reads as belonging to something else. The most-quoted dragon-wing image of the decade is a **composition**, not a creature pose | Drogon lands behind Daenerys and spreads his wings so "the membranous reptilian appendages" read as **her** wings | `[S]` https://www.forbes.com/sites/joshweiss/2019/07/16/emmy-nominated-game-of-thrones-vfx-artist-explains-danys-dragon-wings/ |
| **Cape / cloak drape** | Wings folded but hanging **around and in front of the torso**, hem below the hip | Named trope ("Cape Wings"); real Pteropodid roosting: "it hangs by its hindlimbs and **folds its wings around its body**… the wing will fold around the **front** of the body somewhat" | `[S]` https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/CapeWings ; https://www.tumblr.com/fantasy-anatomy-analyst/636784318876991488/my-previous-post-discussed-the-musculature-of |
| **The tight tuck** | Wings folded completely in, hugging the flank, minimum outline. **Most bats do this**; only Pteropodids and some fruit bats wrap | Same source as above | `[S]` |
| **Grasp / climb / gesture** | The **short digits** open and close independently of the spread — the wing points, grips rock, pins prey | Smaug's whole redesign exists for this: hands to "negotiate his environment and get into **threatening poses**" | `[S]` https://www.fxguide.com/fxfeatured/meet-smaug/ |
| **The shake-off** | Membrane **secondary motion driven by the body**, not by the wing — dragons "shaking snow from their wings" was a stated S8 muscle-dynamics goal | `[S]` https://image-engine.com/case-studies/game-of-thrones-season-8/ | `[S]` |
| **Landing flare / brake** | Membrane at maximum camber, cupped and **visibly filled with air**, tips bent up | Camber runs **0.14 c hovering vs 0.04 c at 7 m/s** — a **3.5× change** — and upward wingtip bending is one of the four named camber controls | prior art §4.5 `[S]` |
| **Ground crawl** | The wing takes weight through the **carpus/short digits**, not the membrane — "bats seem to **crawl into the air with their fingers**" | `[S]` https://www.artofvfx.com/game-of-thrones-season-6-sven-martin-vfx-supervisor-pixomondo/ | `[S]` |

### §2.5 Damage, history and character on the membrane

| Question | Answer | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Do membrane holes heal? | Yes, and **fast** — bat wings have among the fastest healing of any mammal. Wounds of varying size heal in **16–34 days** on average; punch holes in **16–120 days** depending on size, location and species | `[S]` https://iere.org/do-holes-in-bat-wings-heal/ ; https://wildlife.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/jwmg.997 |
| Seasonal rate | 8 mm wounds heal in ~**4 weeks in summer**, ~**7 weeks in winter** | `[S]` https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4295170/ |
| What does healed damage look like? | **Scar tissue, not regeneration.** Scar tissue is **less flexible and elastic** than native membrane; large scars reduce manoeuvrability and increase drag. Rehabbers describe long-lived animals with "wings that look like **lacework** from old injuries" | `[S]` https://iere.org/do-holes-in-bat-wings-heal/ ; https://www.aiwc.ca/blog/bat-wings-flight-and-healing/ |
| The extreme end (boss tier) | Bayle: wings "heavily torn and **shredded, with the bones protruding out of his skin**," paired with a missing leg and an enemy's severed heads embedded in his back. Damage as **narrative**, and it costs him — he no longer has functioning wings | `[S]` https://eldenring.fandom.com/wiki/Bayle_the_Dread |
| How much is too much? | The critique blog treats **decorative** shredding as a defect: shredded wings and stray wing claws "should instead be **smoothed out with extended membrane** for better wing surface area to fly with." Prevalence of "shredded wing membranes on fantasy creatures with bat-like wings" is named as a recurring problem | `[S]` https://fantasy-anatomy-analyst.tumblr.com/post/758407326489247744/hello-what-is-the-best-dragon-depiction-you |
| **The law that falls out** | Damage must be **either healed (closed, scarred, stiffer, lit differently) or costly (fresh, and the flight is worse for it)**. An open hole that never heals and never costs anything is the tell. | `[D]` from the three rows above |
| Best placement | **Trailing edge**: notches read best here — it is the free, fluttering, scalloped edge, so damage there is consistent with how the edge behaves. **Mid-bay**: healed holes read best framed by intact membrane, away from a spar. **Leading edge**: rare and dramatic only — the LE is the actively stiffened structural edge in both real lineages, so a notch there implies serious injury | `[D]` from prior art §4.1/§4.5 + the healing rows |
| Coverage fraction | **`unknown` as a published number.** No studio or illustrator specified a percentage of membrane area that should carry damage | `unknown` — searched studio damage-coverage guidance, none found |

### §2.7 Fold and drape — the pose most artists get wrong

| Rule | Statement | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| The membrane shrinks, it does not pleat | "The whole membrane is like one giant stretchy piece of spandex… when the wing relaxes the membrane **retracts into itself**. The edges don't fold up like a piece of fabric." / "flexible and will simply **shrink inwards, no pleated folding**" | `[S]` rah-bop ; fantasy-anatomy-analyst |
| The fingers **curl together** | "All the fingers kind of curl together" as the wing closes | `[S]` fantasy-anatomy-analyst |
| Two distinct folded silhouettes exist | **Wrap** (Pteropodids, flying foxes — wings fold **around the front of the body**) vs **tight tuck** (most bats — "rest with their wings completely folded in"). These are different characters, not different tightnesses | `[S]` fantasy-anatomy-analyst |
| The fold is a fan closing | Bays overlap progressively; the outline loses one countable feature at a time (as opposed to a single spar, which changes nothing then snaps) | prior art §4.1 `[D]` |
| The drape goes **down and around the flank**, not tidily over the back | The avian fold sits above the back with the tip over the tail base; the membrane fold draws down past the hip like a cloak | prior art §4.6 `[D]`, `[no-assert]` on angles |
| The upstroke is a partial fold | "During flight, birds and bats **fold their wings in a bit on the upstroke**, and this folding plays an important aerodynamic role" | `[S]` fantasy-anatomy-analyst |
| Why artists get it wrong | The half-folded pose is the specific complaint that generated the best source in this stream: folded wing skin "usually cover[s] up half the image and just look[s] wonky." ## §3 Build implications

Each of these is a statement about geometry, silhouette, motion or shading.

**Structure**

1. **Build a wrist cluster, not a wrist joint.** At the carpal apex, place a short forward-and-down
   pointing digit group — 2–3 stubby clawed elements, ~0.15–0.25× the length of the leading spar,
   direction **opposed** to the swept-back spars. Both Smaug (3 short + 3 long) and Drogon (thumb
   forward / fingers back) do this. The forward-vs-back opposition is what makes the wrist read as
   a **hand** at 200 px.
2. **Keep the thumb short and proximal.** Long, palm-adjacent → human hand in a glove. Shortened and
   pushed up the wrist → creature. This was Pixomondo's explicit fix.
3. **Never let a spar tip poke past the membrane hem with a claw on it.** One carpal claw; at most
   one claw at the **leading** spar tip (Rathalos precedent). Zero on the trailing spars.
4. **Weld nothing at the shoulder — overlap it.** A scapular cowl plate that sits over the humeral
   head and stays static in the body frame through the flap, plus a root gusset sweeping aft along
   the flank. The wing's silhouette edge must never be the same line as the body's silhouette edge.
5. **Put the muscle in the torso.** The visible bulge belongs to the back/shoulder/pectoral mass;
   the wing arm itself tapers. A thick wing arm on a slim chest is the fell-beast defect.
6. **Run the plagiopatagium far down the body.** The single praised quality of the best-rated dragon
   wing in the critique literature is how far along the body the membrane stretches. Anchor it to a
   raised flank line or a tail rather than the ankle, so the legs stay free for a rideable.

**Silhouette**

7. **The leading edge is the heaviest line in the wing.** Thicken it, knuckle it, and let it be the
   one edge that stays crisp when everything else blurs.
8. **Keep every joint as a bump in the outline** — elbow, wrist, and one per MCP. A smooth arc from
   shoulder to tip is a hosepipe.
9. **Widest chord inboard**, monotonic taper outboard. Inboard bay ≈ 2× any outboard bay.
10. **Choose the tip and commit**: swept/pointed for a fast read, rounded for a manoeuvrable read.
    Do not average them into a blunt lozenge.
11. **Break the trailing edge three ways**: the scalloped bay concavities (structural), 1–3 healed
    notches (history), and low-amplitude high-frequency flutter (motion).
12. **Desynchronise the two wings.** A few degrees of phase offset and non-identical bay widths.
    Mirror-perfect is the toy read.
13. **Spend budget on membrane area and pattern scale, not on spikes.** Capcom's own shipped
    redesign shortened the spikes, grew the wing, made the membrane more fan-shaped, and made the
    patterns larger — the reverse of the amateur instinct.

**Surface / shading**

14. **The hero lighting state is transmission.** Author the membrane so a light behind it produces a
    warm translucent field with a **darker vascular/fibre network** reading through it, and the
    spars reading as opaque silhouettes inside the glow. This is the state that makes a procedural
    membrane look expensive, and it is free from any texture file.
15. **Keep the membrane's line weight and value contrast below the bones'.** Lighter lines preserve
    thinness. If the hem is as dark as the spar, the wing is sheet metal.
16. **Value tiers must be readable across the bay**: taut root = lit tier, deep cup at ~40% chord =
    shadow tier, hem = transmitted tier. Three tiers minimum.
17. **Never render the membrane matte-opaque uniform.** Fine hair fuzz along the root, vessel
    branching in the bays, scar tissue reading **stiffer and lighter** than the surrounding
    membrane.

**Motion**

18. **Ripple wavelength is a scale dial, not a detail dial.** Set it short with many cycles. Tune it
    to the size you want to claim, then leave it alone.
19. **Every flap must include finger curl on the upstroke.** Shoulder-only rotation is the dead wing.
20. **The membrane must visibly inflate on the downstroke and spill on the upstroke.** Camber changes
    3.5× between hover and cruise — animate the cup, not just the arm.
21. **Blur the free hem and the tip at the extremes of the beat.** Crisp everywhere in every frame is
    a diagram, not a creature.

**Acting — the wing's non-flight repertoire (this is where the wing stops being a prop)**

22. Author at least four **static** wing silhouettes and let the game reach them: **tuck** (minimum
    outline, hugging the flank), **cape drape** (hem below the hip, wrapping forward), **display
    spread** (full horaltic extension, symmetric, maximum projected area), and **mantle** (half-spread,
    hunched forward and down, shoulders rolled, head lowered inside the tent).
23. **The flare is a rate, not a pose.** A deimatic display works because of how fast small becomes
    enormous. Whatever the spread costs in frames, make the transition the fastest thing the
    creature does.
24. **The heraldic frame is a camera decision.** The most famous dragon-wing image of the last decade
    is a spread wing composed **behind** a figure. If the game ever puts the camera in front of the
    rider with the wings behind, that shot exists for free — design the spread pose to be symmetric
    and readable from directly astern.
25. **The wing takes ground weight through the carpus.** Landing and idle poses should put the short
    digit cluster on the ground — "bats crawl into the air with their fingers." The membrane must
    never be the contact surface.

## §4 What this rules out

Kill on sight:

- **A wing whose forward edge is the membrane edge.** Shoulder→wrist with no sheet in front of the
  bone. This is the recognised amateur signature, and it is the same thing §4.1 already rules out
  from anatomy.
- **A wrist that is only a bend.** A smooth two-bone arm with a kink in it and no short digit cluster
  at the apex. Both hero film wings ADD digits there; a bare kink is a hinge, not a hand.
- **Claws on the end of every spar finger**, and **bare finger bone protruding past the membrane hem**
  on anything but the thumb.
- **A folded wing drawn as vertical accordion pleats running across the chord.** The membrane
  retracts into itself; it does not fold like curtain fabric.
- **A folded wing that sits tidily above the back with the tip over the tail base.** That is the avian
  fold on a membrane creature.
- **Opaque matte uniform brown-black membrane** with no transmission state, no vessel structure, and a
  hem line as heavy as the spars. The reference literature is explicit: not leathery at all.
- **A membrane that oscillates in two or three big lazy waves.** It has told the audience the dragon
  is small. Wavelength is the scale cue.
- **A wing arm with visible biceps and deltoids on a slim torso.** The flight mass lives in the back,
  shoulder and pectoral block.
- **A second pair of mirrored shoulder blobs on the back** of a six-limbed dragon. Weta abandoned this
  layout for Smaug; the critique literature calls them "badly photoshopped arms."
- **A hard socket seam where the wing meets the body**, i.e. any frame in which the join is a single
  silhouette edge shared by wing and torso.
- **Ragged decorative shredding that costs the creature nothing** — symmetrical, evenly distributed,
  never healing, flight unaffected. Either scar it closed or make it hurt.
- **Damage concentrated on the leading edge.** The LE is the actively stiffened structural edge; damage
  there implies a crippled animal (Bayle, with bones through the membrane and no functioning wings).
- **A mirror-perfect pair of wings, in phase, with identical bays.**
- **A wing that rotates about the shoulder as a rigid plank**, with no elbow, wrist or finger
  contribution and no finger curl on the upstroke.
- **A wing with only one pose.** If tuck, cape drape, display spread and mantle are not all
  reachable silhouettes, the wing is a propeller.
- **Long spines along the wing bones used as the primary silhouette decoration.** The shipped
  precedent went the other way: shorter spikes, bigger membrane, bigger pattern.
